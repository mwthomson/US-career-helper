# Fractional Executive resume: Presentation and Structure Guide

US English required. Outcome-focused. No generic filler. Tailored to portfolio and fractional career models.

## When to Use a Fractional resume

A fractional executive needs different documents for different contexts. Do not use the same resume everywhere.

| Context | Document | Purpose | Key Differences |
|---------|----------|---------|-----------------|
| Client proposal | Fractional resume | Win an engagement | Emphasize relevant outcomes, concurrent capacity, methodology |
| Platform profile | Abbreviated fractional resume | Attract inbound interest | Headline outcomes, sector focus, availability |
| Job application (permanent) | Traditional resume | Secure employment | Single narrative, ATS-optimized, linear progression |
| LinkedIn | Profile (not a resume) | Attract clients and opportunities | See fractional-linkedin-guide for detail |
| Board or advisory | Board resume | Demonstrate governance capability | Governance experience, sector breadth, independence |

**Rule of thumb:** If the reader is a potential client, use the fractional resume. If the reader is a recruiter filling a permanent role, use a traditional resume adapted for your background.

## Current Portfolio Section

This is the most important section of a fractional resume. It replaces the single "current role" found in traditional resumes and immediately signals that you operate as a fractional professional.

### Structure

```markdown
## Current Portfolio

### Fractional CFO | Kendal Mint (Series B FinTech, 80 employees) | Mar 2025 - Present
2 days/week | Scope: Financial strategy, fundraise preparation, board reporting

- Built Series B financial model and data room; company raised USD 12M at 3x prior valuation
- Reduced monthly close cycle from 15 days to 5 days by implementing automated reporting
- Introduced rolling 13-week cash flow forecasting, improving cash visibility by 60%

### Fractional CFO | HealthTech SaaS (Series A, 40 employees) | Nov 2024 - Present
1 day/week | Scope: Unit economics, pricing strategy, investor relations

- Redesigned pricing model resulting in 22% increase in average contract value
- Prepared board pack framework adopted across quarterly reporting cycle
```

### Formatting Rules for Concurrent Roles

1. **List all active engagements** under a single "Current Portfolio" heading.
2. **State days per week and scope** on a single line beneath each role title.
3. **Use bullet points for outcomes**, not responsibilities. Each bullet should contain a measurable result.
4. **Order by strategic significance**, not by start date or days committed.
5. **Separate completed recent engagements** under a "Recent Engagements" section, with end dates shown.

## Client Confidentiality Handling

Not every client can be named. Use the appropriate level of disclosure.

| Confidentiality Level | Format Example | When to Use |
|----------------------|----------------|-------------|
| Named (with permission) | Fractional CMO, Kendal Mint (Series B FinTech) | Client has given explicit consent to be referenced |
| Anonymized with detail | Fractional CMO, Series B FinTech (70 employees, UK) | No permission, but sector and stage are not identifying |
| Sector only | Fractional CTO, Healthcare SaaS | Client is identifiable by stage/size combination, or NDA applies |
| Confidential | Fractional COO, PE-backed retail business | Strict NDA; provide only the broadest descriptor possible |

**Best practice:** Ask every client at engagement start whether you may reference them by name. Document the answer. When in doubt, anonymize.

## Outcome Focus per Engagement

Every engagement bullet must answer: "What changed because I was there?"

### Weak vs Strong Bullets

| Weak (Activity-Based) | Strong (Outcome-Based) |
|----------------------|----------------------|
| Provided strategic financial advice to the CEO | Built 3-year financial plan that secured USD 8M Series A funding |
| Managed the marketing function on a part-time basis | Increased qualified pipeline by 140% in 6 months through repositioned demand generation strategy |
| Oversaw technology roadmap and team | Reduced time-to-market from 12 weeks to 4 weeks by restructuring sprint processes and eliminating 3 redundant approval layers |
| Advised on people strategy | Designed competency framework and promotion criteria adopted across 120-person organization, reducing voluntary attrition from 28% to 14% |

### Outcome Categories to Quantify

- **Revenue impact:** Pipeline growth, deal size, conversion rates, new revenue streams
- **Cost reduction:** Process efficiency, headcount optimization, vendor renegotiation
- **Risk mitigation:** Compliance achieved, audit outcomes, governance frameworks established
- **Speed improvement:** Time-to-market, reporting cycles, hiring velocity
- **Capability building:** Teams hired, frameworks introduced, systems implemented

## Supporting Your Rate Through resume Positioning

Your resume does not state your rate. However, it must implicitly justify premium pricing through the caliber of outcomes presented.

| Rate-Supporting Signal | How to Embed It |
|----------------------|-----------------|
| Seniority of stakeholders | "Reported directly to CEO and board" or "Presented quarterly to investor group" |
| Scale of impact | Use USD/USD figures, percentages, team sizes, revenue numbers |
| Strategic scope | Describe outcomes at company level, not task level |
| Sector expertise depth | Consistent sector focus across engagements signals specialist value |
| Methodology | Brief mention of frameworks or approaches signals structured thinking |
| Speed of impact | "Within first 90 days" or "Delivered in 8-week sprint" |

## Fractional resume vs Traditional resume: Key Differences

| Element | Traditional resume | Fractional resume |
|---------|---------------|---------------|
| Current role section | Single employer, single title | Multiple concurrent engagements with scope and outcomes |
| Date format | Sequential, no overlapping dates | Overlapping dates are expected and normal |
| Profile summary | Targets a specific role or employer | Targets a type of client and problem |
| Experience depth | 4-6 bullets per role | 2-3 high-impact bullets per engagement; breadth matters |
| Confidentiality | Always named employers | Mix of named, anonymized, and sector-only references |
| Length | 2 pages typical | 2-3 pages acceptable given multiple engagements |
| Tailoring | Per job description | Per client proposal or sector |
| Tone | "I want this role" | "Here is what I deliver" |
| Skills section | Keyword-driven for ATS | Less critical; outcomes demonstrate skills directly |

## Common Mistakes

1. **Listing too many small projects.** If you completed a two-week advisory sprint, it does not warrant its own section. Group minor engagements under a summary line: "Additional advisory engagements across FinTech, EdTech, and HealthTech sectors (2024)."

2. **Not quantifying outcomes.** "Provided strategic advice" tells the reader nothing. Every bullet needs a number, a percentage, a currency figure, or a concrete deliverable.

3. **Mixing employed and fractional roles without clarity.** If you transitioned from permanent employment to fractional work, make the shift explicit. Use section headings like "Current Portfolio" and "Previous Executive Experience" to separate the two clearly.

4. **Using a traditional resume format unchanged.** Concurrent roles listed in standard reverse-chronological format look confusing. The portfolio structure exists to solve this problem.

5. **Omitting engagement scope.** Without scope context, the reader cannot assess whether your experience is relevant. Always state what you were brought in to do.

6. **Failing to show capacity.** Potential clients want to know you have bandwidth. Stating days per week per engagement signals availability without being explicit about selling your time.

7. **Inconsistent confidentiality treatment.** If one client is named and another is anonymized without explanation, it raises questions. Be consistent in your approach and, where possible, note that anonymization is by agreement.
